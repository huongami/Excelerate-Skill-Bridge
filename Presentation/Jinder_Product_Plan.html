01 · Executive summary

The plan on one page.

Australia hosts 680,582 international students, yet 69% of employers report a tech skills shortage. The talent is here; the filters are broken. Jinder matches people to jobs on verified skills, not pedigree — with anonymous profiles, explainable matching and a clear path to every job.

ProductNine new features turn eight user needs into tools: trust first, a clear next step after a low match, skills beyond ICT, and hiring that fits the employer's existing work.
BusinessEmployers pay; talent is seen for free. Premium for employers, a light Premium for talent, and university and training-provider partners. No pay-to-rank.
Marketing“Let the skills speak.” Six product-led moves let people see the value — translate a job title, take a blind hire challenge, audit past rejections.
North starHires made through Jinder · first 5 hire stories by month 6
Revenue10 paying employers · 1 university licence
Trust≥ 80% of candidates “feel safe sharing my CV”

02 · Where Jinder is today

A working platform, ready to grow.

The hackathon build is a complete, two-sided web platform: candidates build a translated skills profile and find jobs; employers post jobs and review anonymous, skill-ranked talent.

172 · 53skills · roles in the Australian ICT taxonomy (ABS ANZSCO)
0names, emails or countries shared with employers

What is missing

  • Reach beyond ICT — many international graduates (like our persona Minh) work in business and operations.
  • A clear next step after a low match — candidates see a gap but not the one skill that opens the most jobs.
  • Fit with employer workflows — no team sharing, ATS export or calendar booking.
  • Real billing and real outcomes — Premium is a demo switch; hiring results are not yet fed back into matching.

Three findings. (1) Trust comes before features — privacy, cost and fairness are the first questions on both sides. (2) Candidates ask “why” and then “what next”. (3) Employers fear extra work more than they want new features.

06 · Business model

Employers pay. Talent is seen for free.

No pay-to-rank. Premium never moves a candidate up a list, and a candidate never pays to be visible. This protects the fairness promise at the heart of the product.
CustomerFreePremium · built todayPremium · next 6 months
Employers
main revenue		Post jobs · top 5 profiles per job · per-skill match · Smart Job Builder hintsSee every profile · invite talent to apply · compare up to 5 profiles · pipeline chartsTalent Alerts · Team Hiring Hub (per seat) · Smart Job Builder benchmarks · Impact Dashboard
CandidatesProfile, translation, applying · match and path to every job · Match Coach “why not” · Application Tracker · Privacy PreviewSkills to learn next · demand for your skillsMatch Coach “what if” · Skill Plan
Partners——University licence (Impact Dashboard for graduate outcomes) · referral fee from training providers (Skill Plan courses)

Why this model

  • The pain sits with employers. The 69% skills shortage is an employer problem, so employers pay for faster, clearer shortlists.
  • Talent supply must stay free. The more candidates join, the more valuable the platform is to employers.
  • Partners add a second and third income that also brings in more candidates (universities) and closes skill gaps (training providers).

Price points are not set in this plan; they will be tested with design partners during months 1–2.

07 · Marketing plan

Let the skills speak.

Show, don't tell. People do not change their minds because we tell them they are biased or overlooked. They change it when they see it. Every move lets people experience the moment when skills are seen before identity — the marketing is the product.

● Show, don't tell
TalentInvisible→Recognised
Employers“No one out there”→“They were here all along”
AudiencePositioning
Candidates“Your skills, recognised. Your identity, protected.” Free, always.
Employers“See the talent your ATS filters out.”
Universities“Proof your graduates get hired.”

Tone: confident, not preachy · warm and human · proof over promise. Not: guilt-tripping employers, a “poor migrants” story, AI hype, or fairness claims without proof.

09 · Beyond six months

Year one: focus, not expansion.

In 12 months, Jinder helps international graduates get fair, skills-based access to entry and mid-level jobs, with a small group of employers and universities who trust it and use it every week.
Months 7–12What we do
ImproveFix what pilot users found hard; make Match Coach and Talent Alerts more accurate using hiring outcomes.
ProveMeasure hires, time saved and repeat use; turn them into case studies.
EarnMake Premium reliable for employers; test one university licence renewal.
One new thingAdd one item from the “Later” list, chosen from pilot feedback (likely career path explorer or verified skill badges).

Deliberately not in year one: national rollout, recruitment agencies, open API, credential-body partnerships, all industries.

Risks and how we reduce them

RiskMitigation
Employers do not payProve value with their own data first (ATS Rejection Audit); start with design partners before billing.
Too few candidates for a strong match poolTalent stays free; product-led moves (Translate My Job Title, Skill Card) are built to spread.
Beyond-ICT taxonomy takes longerStart the data work in month 1 and add one area (business & operations) before any other.
Not enough hires to train the flywheelCollect hiring outcome feedback from day one; keep rule-based matching as the reliable base.
A privacy or fairness incidentZero-PII design, consent-only identity, no pay-to-rank, humans make every decision; review against the Privacy Act before launch.
