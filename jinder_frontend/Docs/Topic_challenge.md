# Topic Challenge

## Track 1: Future of Work and International Talent

### Focus

Help international students and skilled migrants access meaningful career opportunities, while helping Australian employers identify, develop, and retain diverse talent.

### Example challenge areas

- Skills and job matching
- Australian career pathways
- Credential recognition
- Workforce planning
- Inclusive recruitment
- Employee onboarding and retention

## The Problem

### 1. Australia continues to attract global talent

- **680,582** international students studied in Australia from January to May 2026.
- Higher Education enrolments increased **2%** compared with January-May 2025.
- Overall international student numbers decreased **6.9%** compared with January-May 2025.

Australia continues to attract a growing international talent pool through its higher education system and skilled migration programs. Between 2022 and 2024, the number of international students increased by approximately 38%, reaching more than 850,000 students nationwide. While this growth contributes significantly to Australia's economy and workforce, it also increases the need for more effective pathways that help international talent transition successfully into employment.

The 680,582 international students recorded between January and May 2026, alongside continued year-on-year growth in Higher Education enrolments, reflect Australia's continued ability to attract highly educated international talent.

Source: Australian Government Department of Education (2026).

### 2. Employer expectations are changing

- **69%** of Australian employers report difficulty finding skilled talent (ManpowerGroup, 2024).
- **92%** of technology executives believe AI capability will become an essential workforce skill within the next five years (KPMG Global Tech Report, 2026).

Employers increasingly seek graduates who can combine technical expertise with AI literacy and digital capability. Australian employers also face difficulties identifying transferable skills, evaluating international experience, and connecting with suitably qualified candidates despite ongoing workforce shortages.

> **The real challenge:** Australia is not lacking talent. It is facing a growing gap between graduate capability and workforce expectations in an AI-enabled economy. The challenge is no longer attracting talent; it is helping talent succeed.

### Barriers for international talent

- Understanding local recruitment processes
- Translating overseas qualifications
- Building professional networks
- Gaining relevant Australian experience

### Challenges for employers

- Identifying transferable skills
- Evaluating international experience
- Connecting with suitable candidates
- Retaining diverse talent

These issues create a disconnect between available talent and labour-market demand.

## Challenge Question

> How might we use AI, data, and digital technology to help international students and skilled migrants access meaningful career opportunities, while helping Australian employers identify, develop, and retain diverse talent?

## Your Challenge

Design an AI-powered solution that improves employment outcomes for international talent while helping Australian organisations better identify, develop, and retain skilled people.

The solution should demonstrate clear value for users, be technically feasible, and address a genuine workforce challenge in Australia.

## Challenge Areas

Participants may focus on one or more of the following areas:

1. Career navigation
2. Skills recognition and job matching
3. Employability and career readiness
4. Employer recruitment and workforce planning

## Example Solution Directions

| Direction | Description |
| --- | --- |

| Skills translation platform | Translate overseas skills and experience into Australian standards. |

## Challenge Guidelines

### Challenge expectations

Teams do not need to solve every part of the challenge. Each team should identify:

- One clear problem
- One main target user
- One realistic solution that can be developed within the hackathon

Strong submissions will demonstrate:

- A clearly defined user problem
- Evidence that the problem is important
- A focused and realistic solution
- A working prototype or convincing proof of concept
- A clear explanation of how the technology works
- Consideration of implementation, privacy, fairness, and accessibility
- A measurable benefit for users, organisations, or communities

Effective hackathon challenge briefs connect a broad issue with a specific user, local context, practical constraints, and supporting resources. Humanitarian challenge examples use individual personas and a clear problem scenario to guide participants. ASEF challenge examples similarly move from a broad theme to a clearly defined local problem, target population, and intended impact.

### What teams can build

The solution may take the form of:

- An AI agent or assistant
- A web or mobile application
- A data analytics dashboard
- A recommendation or matching system
- An automation tool
- A predictive analytics solution
- A digital service or platform
- A working prototype supported by a clear implementation plan

Teams are encouraged to use AI, data, and digital tools where they add genuine value. Using AI merely for the sake of including it will not result in a higher score. Teams should clearly explain why the selected technology is appropriate for the problem.

### Key constraints

All teams should consider the following requirements.

#### Responsible use of AI and data

- Privacy and data protection
- Bias and fairness
- Transparency
- Security
- Appropriate human oversight
- Responsible use of personal and employment information

#### Accessibility and inclusion

Solutions should be usable by people with different:

- Cultural and language backgrounds
- Levels of digital confidence
- Education and professional experience
- Accessibility requirements

#### Practical implementation

Solutions should be realistic for an Australian employer, university, training provider, or community organisation to implement.

Teams should avoid relying on:

- Data that would not realistically be available
- Unreasonable budgets
- Technology that cannot be explained
- Fully automated, high-impact decisions without human review
